1 Course Policies
1. Assignments
You will receive full marks as long as successful submissions are made by the corre-
sponding submission deadlines, which are enforced strictly.
The format of your programming assignments is identical to that of the subsequent
programming tests. Therefore, it is in your best interest to follow the export and
submission process (for which you will be expected to complete alone during the
programming tests) and submit work that:
– complies with the instructions (so as to avoid penalties when you submit for
grading in the actual programming tests); and
– represents your true and best attempt.
Therationalesofthispolicyarethat:1)youcanrest assured that you will not lose
any marks from assignments (as long as you submit them by the deadlines);
and 2) you can just focus on the learning by seeking help from colleagues,
TAs, and your section instructor without worrying about violating the aca-
demic honesty policy.
Please do not abuse this policy: you are still 100% responsible for acquiring
the intended understandings and skills from these assignments. Be advised
that later scheduled (written and programming) tests, as well as the final
written exam, will be based on these assignments, so if you chose not to learn
the materials responsibly (e.g., relying much on your colleagues, submitting incomplete
work and only intending to look at solutions when they are made available), you would
risk poor performance in subsequent tests and the exam.
2. Assignment/Test Deadlines
Stringent deadlinesareimposedonallscheduledin-personwritten tests, scheduled
in-person programming tests, and assignments (to be submitted remotely via the
web submit to the EECS server).
An in-person, written exam will be scheduled by the registrar office to take place
during the exam period.
It is your sole responsibility to meet all these deadlines.
3. Test Dates and Locations
For each test, it is your sole responsibility for ensuring that you are available to take
the test (during the chosen lecture or tutorial time of your enrolled section).
The exact time and location of each test will be announced at least one week prior to
each test.

All written & programming tests are to be completed individually.
When a scheduled test takes place between different sections, until all test sessions
conclude, it is considered a violation of academic integrity if you communicate in
any way, shape, or form with others about the test(s) already given.

7 Course Description
This course is intended to teach students the fundamental techniques in the design of algo-
rithmsandtheanalysisoftheircomputationalcomplexity. Eachofthesetechniquesisapplied
to a number of widely used and practical problems.
At the end of this course, a student will be able to:
• choose algorithms appropriate for many common computational problems;
• exploit constraints and structure to design efficient algorithms; and
• select appropriate tradeoffs for speed and space.
Weekly three-hour lectures and 1.5-hour scheduled mandatory tutorials.
Topics covered may include:
• a review of fundamental data structures,
• asymptotic notation,
• solving recurrences,
• sorting and order statistics,
• divide-and-conquer approaches,
• dynamic programming,
• greedy method,
• divide-and-conquer algorithms,
• amortization approaches,
• graph algorithms, and
• the theory of NP-completeness.
8 Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)
Upon completion of the course, students are expected to be able to:
Clo1 Choose an appropriate algorithm to solve a given computational problem, and
justify that choice.
Clo2 Design new algorithms using a variety of techniques (recursion, greedy algo-
rithm, dynamic programming, backtracking).
Clo3 Prove correctness of an algorithm using pre- and post-conditions and loop
invariants.
Clo4 Prove bounds on the running time of an algorithm.
Clo5 Apply standard graph algorithms to a variety of problems.

15 In-Person Session Protocol
While lectures are recorded for review and attendance is encouraged, if you do decide to
attend an in-person session (e.g., lecture, tutorial), you are expected to follow
the rules below to keep the classroom a respectful and focused space for learning.
– Avoid side conversations during lecture — regardless of how quietly you believe
you are speaking, side conversations disrupt the instructor’s focus and the class.
– Mobile phones are to be put away at all times, except during the iClicker
attendance check before the short break.
– Headphones are to be put away at all times.
– Laptops are to be used only for taking notes or following along with lecture
materials. Using them to complete labs or assignments — whether for this course or
for another — is disrespectful to both your instructor and fellow classmates.
When these rules are not followed, it distracts your instructor and your classmates.
– Your instructor does not have the energy to police the class every minute. When they
intervene, it is because the behaviour has become disruptive enough for them to act.
– When you are approached, do not argue that “others were doing the same thing”
or “I was not doing other things on my laptop” — your instructor will address
according to how they genuinely perceive the disruption in the moment,
and that judgment is final.
– This section of the syllabus counts as a general warning. In some cases, your
instructor may give an individual reminder, but they may also directly ask a student to
leave without a further warning. That decision is not open to debate during
class time.
– Refusing to leave only wastes the time of your fellow students who come to focus and
learn; if that happens, your instructor may involve campus security as a last resort
to maintain a safe and focused learning environment.
These rules exist to protect the learning experience of students who attend class prepared
and focused.

19 (Tentative) Lecture Topics
Whereas the pace will be adjusted according to the class dynamics, the following topics are
planned to be covered:
– Generics in Java: Linked Lists and Trees
– Growth of Functions and Asymptotic Running-Time Analysis
– Dynamic Arrays and Amortized Running-Time Analysis
– Balanced Binary Search Trees and Tree Rotations
– Priority Queue ADT, Heap Definitions, and Heap Operations
– Divide-and-Conquer Algorithms and Recurrence Relations
– Graph ADT: Terminology and Traversal Algorithms
– Graph ADT: Shortest Paths, Topological Sorts, and Spanning Trees
– Graph ADT: Implementation Strategies
– Greedy Algorithms and Loop Invariants for Iterative Algorithms
– Algorithm Correctness Arguments, including Mathematical Induction
– Dynamic Programming Principles
– NP-Completeness and Reductions (time permitting)
The order, depth, pacing, and emphasis of these topics may change as the semester pro-
gresses, depending on class progress, questions, and instructional needs. This list is not a
contract, and it should not be interpreted as a guarantee of coverage depth, exam emphasis,
or exclusion of related material discussed in lectures, tutorials, or assignments.
